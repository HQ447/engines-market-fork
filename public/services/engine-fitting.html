Before you fit

Signs You Need Professional Engine Fitting

If you already have an engine and need it fitted, professional installation is essential. Here's why you shouldn't DIY.

⚠️ Engine alignment
Misaligned engine can cause vibration, premature wear, and damage to engine mounts and drivetrain components.
⚠️ Correct torque settings
Incorrectly torqued bolts - especially head bolts, flywheel bolts, and main bearing bolts - can cause catastrophic failure.
⚠️ Fluid filling
Incorrect oil grade, overfilling or underfilling can cause immediate engine damage.
⚠️ Cooling system bleeding
Air pockets in the cooling system can cause localised overheating and head gasket failure.
⚠️ Wiring and sensors
Incorrectly connected sensors can cause engine management issues, warning lights, and drivability problems.
⚠️ Exhaust connections
Poorly fitted exhaust can cause leaks, MOT failure, and reduced performance.
⚠️ Drivetrain alignment
Misaligned clutch, gearbox, or propshaft can cause vibration and premature wear.

Risks & caveats

Risks & Caveats of Customer-Supplied Engines

When you supply your own engine, you accept certain risks. Here's what you need to know.

Risk 1: No Warranty on the Engine

The fitter does not warrant your engine. If the engine has internal damage - even if it was supplied as "tested" - the fitter is not responsible.

Mitigation: Have the engine inspected by a specialist before installation. Or, consider using a supply-and-fit service where the supplier warranties the engine.

Risk 2: Compatibility Issues

You may have sourced an engine that is not compatible with your vehicle - different engine code, different suffix, different emissions spec.

Mitigation: Verify the engine code matches exactly - including the suffix. If you're unsure, have the fitter check before installation.

Risk 3: Missing Ancillaries

Your engine may be missing ancillary components - turbo, injectors, alternator, starter, wiring harness.

Mitigation: Confirm with the supplier exactly what is included. Budget for missing ancillaries.

Risk 4: Fitment Quality

The fitter is responsible for the installation - but if the engine fails due to internal damage, you pay.

Mitigation: Choose a fitter with good reviews. Ask them to check the engine before installation.

"We always recommend a supply-and-fit service over customer-supplied engines. The cost difference is £300-£600 - but if the customer-supplied engine fails, they pay for removal and refitting again. That's £1,000+ extra. The supply-and-fit service gives them warranty coverage on the whole job."
- UK Engine Specialist Interview #18 [EM-INTERVIEW]

Frequently asked questions

Engine Fitting FAQs

How much does engine fitting cost- +
Engine fitting costs vary by vehicle complexity: 4-cylinder FWD £800-£1,200, 6-cylinder RWD £1,000-£1,800, V8 / 4WD / SUV £1,500-£2,500, commercial / van £1,800-£3,000.
What's included in the fitting cost- +
Fitting typically includes: removal of the old engine, installation of your engine, basic consumables (oil, coolant, filters), basic gaskets and seals, post-installation testing, and disposal of the old engine.
What's NOT included- +
Engine supply, warranty on the engine, collection/delivery of your vehicle, clutch/DMF, water pump/thermostat, air-con regas, ancillary parts, and unforeseen repairs discovered during fitting.
Do you warranty customer-supplied engines- +
No - the fitter warranties their labour, not your engine. If the engine fails due to internal damage, you are responsible. This is why we recommend supply-and-fit for most customers.
How long does engine fitting take- +
Labour typically takes 8-15 hours (1-3 days) for a standard engine, or 15-25 hours (3-5 days) for a complex installation. The average timeline from vehicle arrival to completion is 2-5 days.
Can I supply my own engine- +
Yes - this is a labour-only service. You supply the engine, we connect you with fitters who can install it. However, we recommend having the engine inspected before installation.
What if my engine is faulty- +
If the engine is faulty, you will need to source a replacement and pay for removal and refitting again. This is why we recommend supply-and-fit for most customers - it includes warranty coverage.
Do I need to tell the DVLA and my insurer- +
DVLA - update the engine number on your V5C [DVLA, 2025]. Insurer - you should check with your insurer. Some policies require notification of major mechanical changes, while others may not.
