Definition · BS AU257

What "Remanufactured" Actually Means

BS AU257:2002 - The British Standard for Remanufacturing

"A remanufactured engine is exactly that - an engine which has been returned to the manufacturer's specification to provide levels of performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine."

The Remanufacturing Process

  1. Full strip-down - Engine completely dismantled to component level
  2. Clean and inspect - All components cleaned, measured, and inspected
  3. Replace all wear components - Pistons, rings, bearings, seals, gaskets, timing components - all new
  4. Machine to original tolerances - Block, cylinder head, crankshaft, camshaft - all machined to factory spec
  5. Reassemble - Using new components and OEM-spec parts
  6. Test - Dyno-tested to ensure performance matches original engine

Certification

Why BS AU257:2002 Matters

BS AU257:2002 is the British Standard Code of Practice for the remanufacture of automotive components - specifically engines.

What the Standard Requires:

  • Manufacturer's specification - The engine must be returned to the original manufacturer's tolerances and specifications.
  • New components - All wearing components (pistons, rings, bearings, seals, gaskets) must be new - not reused.
  • Testing - The engine must be tested to ensure performance matches the original.
  • Traceability - All components must be traceable to source.
  • Quality management - The remanufacturer must have ISO 9001:2015 quality management.

Frequently asked questions

Remanufactured Engine FAQs

What is a remanufactured engine- +
A remanufactured engine is returned to the manufacturer's original specification - providing levels of performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine. It is certified to BS AU257:2002, the British Standard for remanufacturing.
How much does a remanufactured engine cost- +
Remanufactured engines cost £3,500-£6,500 supply only or £5,500-£9,000 fitted. That's 30-40% more than reconditioned, but 40% less than a new OEM engine.
What's the difference between remanufactured and reconditioned- +
A reconditioned engine has worn parts replaced and is made serviceable. A remanufactured engine is returned to factory specification - all wear components are new, the engine is machined to original tolerances, and it is certified to BS AU257:2002.
What is BS AU257:2002- +
BS AU257:2002 is the British Standard Code of Practice for the remanufacture of automotive components. It requires the engine to be returned to the manufacturer's original specification, with all wear components replaced and performance equivalent to the original.
Who should buy a remanufactured engine- +
Commercial fleets (minimising downtime), classic car owners (preserving originality), insurance/warranty jobs (like-new replacement), and owners of very high-value vehicles (£30,000+).
How long does a remanufactured engine take- +
Lead time is typically 7-14 days. This includes sourcing a core, stripping, machining, rebuilding, and testing. Remanufacturers often hold stock of common engines.
What warranty comes with a remanufactured engine- +
Typically 12-24 months, parts and labour. This is equivalent to the warranty on a new engine - unlike reconditioned engines which typically have shorter warranties.
Do I need to tell the DVLA and my insurer- +
DVLA - update the engine number on your V5C [DVLA, 2025]. Insurer - you should check with your insurer. Some policies require notification of major mechanical changes, while others may not.
