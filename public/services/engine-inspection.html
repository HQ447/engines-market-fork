Two Use Cases

Two Use Cases - When You Need an Engine Inspection

Engine inspections serve two distinct purposes. Choose the one that fits your situation.

🔍
Use Case 1: Pre-Purchase Inspection
You're considering buying a used car. You want to know if the engine is healthy before you commit.
  • Compression test - shows cylinder health
  • Borescope inspection - reveals internal cylinder wear
  • Visual inspection - condition of ancillaries, belts, hoses
  • Wear analysis - can reveal mileage discrepancies
  • Future cost estimate - likely repairs and lifespan
Best for: Used car buyers, private sales, auction purchases, classic car buyers
⚠️
Use Case 2: Failure Inspection
Your engine has failed or is showing symptoms. You need to understand the extent of the damage before deciding on repair or replacement.
  • Failure mode analysis - identifies what caused the failure
  • Borescope - shows extent of internal damage
  • Oil analysis - detects debris contamination
  • Repair vs replace assessment - determines if repair is possible
  • Cost-effective recommendation - written recommendation
Best for: Owners with unexplained failure, warning lights, or unusual noises

Inspection Service

What Inspection Includes

A professional engine inspection covers these critical components.

👁️ Visual Inspection
External condition, belts, hoses, leaks, corrosion, ancillaries
Identifies visible issues - leaks, worn belts, damaged components
📊 Compression Test
Cylinder pressure - all cylinders measured and compared
Reveals worn piston rings, damaged valves, head gasket issues
💨 Leak-Down Test
How well each cylinder holds pressure - identifies leakage path
Pinpoints exact cause of compression loss - valves, rings, head gasket
🔬 Borescope Inspection
Internal view of cylinders, piston crowns, cylinder walls
Reveals scoring, damage, carbon buildup - hidden wear
🧪 Oil Analysis (optional)
Metal content, contamination, viscosity
Detects internal wear (metal particles), coolant contamination, fuel dilution
💧 Coolant Condition
Colour, contamination, pH level
Indicates head gasket failure or cooling system issues
⚙️ Ancillary Condition
Alternator, starter, turbo (if accessible), water pump
Condition of supporting components - likely future failures
⛓️ Timing Component Check
Condition of timing belt/chain, tensioners, guides (if accessible)
Prevents catastrophic timing failure
📄 Written Report
Findings, pass/fail status, photos, recommendations
You get a clear, actionable summary

What "Pass," "Advisory," and "Fail" Mean

✅ Pass
Engine is in good condition - no significant issues
Proceed with purchase / continue driving
⚠️ Advisory
Minor issues found - some wear, minor leaks
Budget for future repairs - proceed with caution
❌ Fail
Significant issues found - internal damage, major leaks
Walk away (pre-purchase) or replace (failure inspection)

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about engine inspection

How much does an engine inspection cost- ×
Basic inspection £100-£150, full inspection £150-£250, comprehensive inspection £200-£300, classic/specialist £250-£400. Prices vary by vehicle type and region.
What's the difference between diagnostics and inspection- +
Diagnostics reads trouble codes from the ECU - finds electrical and sensor faults. Inspection is a physical examination - compression test, borescope, oil analysis. They're complementary services.
When should I get an engine inspection- +
Before buying a used car, after an unexplained failure, before a major trip, or when buying a classic car. If you're uncertain about the engine's condition, an inspection is the best investment.
What does the inspection report include- +
Visual inspection results, compression test figures, leak-down test results, borescope images (if performed), oil analysis results (if selected), condition assessment (pass/advisory/fail), and recommendations.
Can I use the inspection for a private car sale- +
Yes - a pre-purchase inspection is ideal for private sales. The buyer can see the condition of the engine before committing. It's also useful for the seller to demonstrate the vehicle's condition.
Do I need to bring the car to the workshop- +
Yes - the inspection requires physical access to the vehicle. The specialist will need to run the engine, perform compression tests, and use borescopes and other equipment.
What if the inspection finds a problem- +
If the problem is repairable, get repair quotes. If replacement is needed, get replacement quotes. If you're buying the car, the inspection gives you leverage to negotiate the price or walk away.
Can I do an engine inspection myself- +
You can do a basic visual inspection yourself. But compression tests, leak-down tests, and borescope inspections require specialist tools and expertise. A professional inspection is more thorough and provides a written report.
