Definition

What Is a Reconditioned Engine-

A reconditioned engine is a used engine that has been fully dismantled, cleaned, inspected, machined where necessary, and rebuilt with new wearing parts. It is not simply a "cleaned up" used engine - it is a professionally rebuilt unit with documented work and a warranty.

Step by step

The Reconditioning Process

A proper reconditioning process takes time. Here's what happens at each stage.

01
Strip - Full Dismantling
Engine completely stripped: cylinder head, pistons, crankshaft, camshafts, timing components, oil pump, water pump, sensors.
02
Clean - Thorough Degreasing
Thermal cleaning (cast iron), shot blasting (external), ultrasonic cleaning (oil galleries), chemical degreasing (aluminium).
03
Inspect - Measure Everything
Cylinder bore wear, crankshaft journal wear, camshaft lobe wear, valve guide wear, block flatness, crack detection.
04
Machine - Restore to Specification
Cylinders honed/bored, crankshaft journals ground, cylinder head skimmed, valve seats cut, valve guides replaced.
05
Rebuild - Replace and Reassemble
New piston rings, main bearings, big-end bearings, thrust washers, timing chain/belt, oil pump, water pump, gaskets, seals, core plugs.
06
Test - Bench Test and Run
Oil pressure checked, compression tested, leak-down test, engine run (cold & hot) to check for leaks, noises, and correct operation.

"A proper reconditioning process takes time. When we strip an engine, we find things that weren't obvious from the outside - cracks, worn bearings, damaged oil galleries. That's why we never skip the inspection step. It's what separates a real reconditioned engine from a 'cleaned up' used engine."

✅ What the Warranty Covers (Typically)

  • Internal mechanical failure of the engine assembly (block, cylinder head, crankshaft, pistons, connecting rods, camshaft, valves, timing components)
  • Parts and labour (for the covered failure)

⚠️ What the Warranty Does NOT Cover (Typically)

  • Ancillaries (alternator, starter, air conditioning compressor, turbocharger, injectors, fuel pump) - unless specifically stated
  • Belts and hoses, gaskets and seals (if they fail after installation)
  • Damage caused by incorrect fitting, inadequate lubrication, or overheating after installation
  • Damage caused by neglect or failure to maintain
  • Consequential damage (e.g., if a component failure causes damage to other parts)

The Golden Rule: Always read the warranty terms before committing. A warranty is only as good as the supplier standing behind it. If a supplier cannot tell you the claims process, the exclusions, and the duration, they are not offering a real warranty.

Questions to Ask About Warranty

  1. What exactly does the warranty cover- (Parts only- Parts and labour-)
  2. What is the duration- (6 months- 12 months- 24 months-)
  3. What is the claims process- (Who do I contact- What documentation do I need-)
  4. What are the exclusions- (Gaskets- Seals- Ancillaries- Consequential damage-)
  5. Is the warranty transferable if I sell the vehicle-
  6. What happens if I have a problem 6 months and 1 day after installation-

Risks

Reconditioned Engine Risks & Caveats

Reconditioned engines are the most popular choice - but they are not without risks.

Risk 1: Quality Varies by Supplier
Some suppliers cut corners - skipping machining, using cheap parts, or rushing the rebuild. Mitigation: Choose a supplier with clear documentation, photos of the rebuild, and 4.0+ star reviews.
Risk 2: "Reconditioned" vs "Rebuilt" Confusion
Terms are used interchangeably in the UK. Mitigation: Always ask for a detailed written specification of exactly what work will be performed.
Risk 3: Warranty Exclusions
Labour may not be covered, ancillaries excluded, consequential damage not included. Mitigation: Read the warranty terms carefully. Ask specifically about labour coverage and consequential damage.
Risk 4: Fitment Quality
Poor fitting can cause oil leaks, coolant leaks, premature wear, or immediate failure. Mitigation: Use a reputable garage with experience in your engine type. Ask the supplier for fitting recommendations.
Risk 5: Vehicle Value Mismatch
If your vehicle is worth under £4,000, the cost of a reconditioned engine may exceed the vehicle's value. Mitigation: Use the vehicle value rule in Section 7. Consider a used engine or scrapping.

Frequently asked questions

Reconditioned Engine FAQs

How much does a reconditioned engine cost in the UK- +
Based on 17,365 reconditioned engine quote requests in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reconditioned engines cost £2,100-£3,500 supply only or £3,500-£5,500 fitted. Prices vary by engine type, vehicle segment, and regional labour rates.
What's the difference between reconditioned and rebuilt- +
In the UK engine industry, many suppliers use "reconditioned" and "rebuilt" interchangeably. There is no legal standard that separates these terms. A true rebuild often includes upgraded components, but a high-quality recondition from a reputable supplier may be indistinguishable. Always ask for a detailed written specification.
What warranty comes with a reconditioned engine- +
Reconditioned engines typically carry a 6-12 month warranty covering parts and labour on internal mechanical failure. Labour coverage is a key differentiator from used engine warranties (which are parts-only). Always read the warranty terms before committing.
Is a reconditioned engine worth the extra cost over a used one- +
For vehicles worth over £5,000 that you plan to keep 2+ years - yes. Reconditioned units carry a 6-12 month warranty and have new wearing parts. Our data shows 70.45% of buyers choose reconditioned for the warranty alone.
How long does a reconditioned engine take- +
Lead time is typically 3-7 days. This includes sourcing the core engine, stripping, cleaning, inspecting, machining, rebuilding, and testing. Some suppliers offer "off-the-shelf" reconditioned units that can ship in 24-48 hours.
Can I fit a reconditioned engine myself- +
We strongly recommend professional fitting. Reconditioned engines are precision units that require correct installation - incorrect fitting can void the warranty and cause premature failure.
What should I ask a reconditioned engine supplier before buying- +
Ask for: 1. Detailed written specification of work performed. 2. Photos of the rebuild. 3. Warranty terms (length, parts & labour, exclusions). 4. What ancillaries are included. 5. Lead time. 6. Fitting recommendations.
Do I need to tell the DVLA and my insurer- +
DVLA - update the engine number on your V5C [DVLA, 2025]. Insurer - you should check with your insurer. Some policies require notification of major mechanical changes, while others may not.
