Inspection service

What's included in a standalone engine inspection

A professional inspection covers the critical mechanical condition of your engine. Here's what a typical inspection includes.

🔍 External Visual Inspection
External condition, casing cracks, corrosion, ancillaries condition, sealing surfaces - identifies visible damage.
📊 Compression Test
Cylinder pressure measured for all cylinders (engine rotated on stand) - reveals worn piston rings, damaged valves, head gasket issues.
💨 Leak-Down Test
How well each cylinder holds pressure - pinpoints exact cause of compression loss (valves, rings, head gasket).
🔬 Borescope Inspection
Internal view of cylinders, piston crowns, cylinder walls - reveals scoring, damage, carbon buildup, overheating marks.
🛢️ Oil Condition Check (Basic)
Colour, smell, presence of metal particles - indicates internal wear, contamination, or neglect.
🧪 Oil Analysis (Optional - extra cost)
Metal content, contamination, viscosity - detects internal wear, coolant contamination, fuel dilution.
⏱️ Timing Component Check
Condition of timing belt/chain, tensioners, guides (if accessible) - prevents catastrophic timing failure.
🔧 Ancillary Assessment
Inspection of ancillaries (turbo, injectors, alternator, starter) if still attached - identifies which may need replacement.
📄 Written Report
Written findings, pass/fail status, recommendations, estimated repair costs - a clear, actionable summary.

What "Pass," "Advisory," and "Fail" Mean

✅ Pass

Engine is in good condition - compression strong, no visible damage.

→ Proceed with purchase or fitting
⚠️ Advisory

Minor issues found - some wear, minor corrosion, perished seals.

→ Proceed with caution - budget for minor work before fitting
❌ Fail

Significant issues found - scored cylinders, low compression, cracks.

→ Walk away (pre-purchase) or do not fit (pre-fit)

When to walk away

The "Fail" scenarios

Some inspection findings mean you should walk away - every time.

❌
Low compression on one or more cylinders
Indicates worn rings, damaged valves, or head gasket failure.
→ Walk away - rebuild cost may exceed engine value
❌
Scoring on cylinder walls (borescope)
Internal damage - requires rebore or replacement.
→ Walk away - not economically repairable
❌
Crack in engine block or cylinder head
Structural damage - cannot be repaired.
→ Walk away - engine is scrap
❌
Metal particles in oil (oil analysis)
Internal wear - bearings, rings, or other components failing.
→ Walk away - full rebuild likely needed
❌
Signs of overheating (discoloured components)
Engine has been overheated - risk of warping and future failure.
→ Walk away - may appear fine but fail shortly after fitting
❌
Corrosion in coolant passages
Cooling system neglect - risk of overheating and failure.
→ Walk away - may have hidden damage
⚠️
Timing component damage (visible)
Chain/belt worn - risk of catastrophic failure.
→ Walk away - unless the price reflects the risk

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about standalone engine inspection

How much does a standalone engine inspection cost- ×
Basic visual £100-£150, standard £150-£250, full £200-£350, comprehensive £300-£500. Prices vary by engine type and region. [EM-QUOTE]
What's the difference between this service and the vehicle-based inspection- +
Vehicle-based inspection is for engines fitted in a vehicle. This service is for standalone engines on a stand or pallet - for used engine buyers, importers, and pre-fit verification.
When should I get a standalone engine inspection- +
Before buying a used engine online, before paying for fitting, or if you've already bought an engine and want to verify its condition before installation.
What does the inspection include- +
External visual inspection, compression test, leak-down test, borescope inspection, oil condition check, timing component check (if accessible), and a written report with pass/fail status.
What if the engine fails the inspection- +
If pre-purchase - walk away. If pre-fit - do not fit. The inspection report gives you the evidence to negotiate a refund or avoid wasting labour costs.
Can I get the inspection done before I buy the engine- +
Yes - if the seller is willing to allow the inspection. Some sellers may not allow it - that's a red flag in itself.
Do I need to send my engine to the workshop- +
Yes - the engine needs to be physically at the workshop for inspection. You can deliver it or arrange transport.
What if the engine is still installed in a vehicle- +
Use the vehicle-based engine inspection service instead - it's designed for engines fitted in cars.
