Warranty Types

Parts-only vs Parts and Labour

Most engine suppliers offer two main types of warranty. The difference is significant.

Warranty type What it covers What it does NOT cover Typical duration
Parts-only The replacement engine itself (if it fails due to manufacturing defect) Labour to remove and refit the engine; diagnosis; fluids; gaskets; consequential damage 1-6 months
Parts and labour The replacement engine plus the labour cost to remove the faulty unit and refit the replacement Consequential damage (e.g., damage to other components caused by engine failure); towing; rental car 6-24 months

Key question to ask: “If the engine fails within the warranty period, who pays for the labour to swap it out again-”

If the answer is anything other than “the supplier covers labour as well”, you have a parts-only warranty.

Coverage

Common warranty inclusions

What is typically covered

Most engine warranties (especially on reconditioned and rebuilt units) cover:

  • Internal lubricated parts - crankshaft, connecting rods, pistons, piston rings, camshafts, valves, valve springs, rocker arms, lifters, oil pump
  • Timing components - timing chain, belt, tensioners, guides (if they fail due to manufacturing defect)
  • Gaskets and seals - but only if they fail as a direct result of another covered component failing (not as standalone items)

Important: Coverage always depends on the supplier’s exact warranty terms. Always read the written document - not just what you are told over the phone.

Exclusions

Common warranty exclusions

What is typically NOT covered

Almost no engine warranty covers everything. These exclusions are standard across the industry:

Exclusion Why it is usually excluded
Gaskets and sealsConsidered wear items. Head gaskets, valve cover gaskets, sump gaskets, crank seals, cam seals - these are almost never covered as standalone failures.
Labour to refitParts-only warranties specifically exclude labour. Even some parts-and-labour warranties may limit the labour rate (e.g., £60 per hour, not the garage’s actual rate).
Consequential damageIf the engine fails and damages the turbo, DPF, catalytic converter, or radiator - those parts are not covered. Only the engine itself.
Incorrect installationIf the engine was fitted incorrectly (wrong oil, wrong torque settings, damaged sensors), the warranty is void.
Neglect or abuseRunning without oil, ignoring warning lights, overheating, racing, towing beyond capacity - all void warranties.
ModificationsRemapping, tuning, performance upgrades - most suppliers exclude these immediately.
ConsumablesOil, coolant, filters, drive belts - not covered even if the engine is replaced under warranty.
Collection or deliveryTransport costs to return a failed engine or to receive a replacement are rarely covered.
Diagnosis timeThe cost to diagnose the failure is usually not covered - only the repair or replacement itself.

Ask before you buy: “Can you send me a written copy of your full warranty exclusions-”

If a supplier hesitates or says “it’s standard”, that is a red flag.

Duration

Warranty duration - what to expect by engine condition

Longer warranties generally mean the supplier has more confidence in their product. But duration alone is not everything - you also need to check what is covered.

Engine condition Typical warranty length Typical coverage type
Used engine (tested, warranted)1-6 monthsParts-only (rarely includes labour)
Reconditioned engine6-12 months (sometimes 24 months on premium units)Parts and labour (check the labour cap)
Rebuilt engine (your own engine sent away)12-24 monthsParts and labour (higher confidence from the rebuilder)
Remanufactured engine (BS AU257 standard)12 months unlimited mileageParts and labour (industry standard)
New OEM engine12-36 monthsParts and labour (dealer warranty)

Note: These are typical ranges based on the UK market. Your supplier’s actual terms may differ. Always confirm in writing.

Checklist

Questions you must ask every supplier

Copy these questions and ask every supplier before you agree to anything.

Before you buy:

  • “What exactly does your warranty cover - parts only, or parts and labour-”
  • “How long does the warranty last - from the date of invoice-”
  • “Is there a mileage limit (e.g., 12,000 miles per year)-”
  • “What is excluded from your warranty- Please send me a written list.”
  • “If the engine fails, who pays for the labour to remove and refit it-”
  • “Is there a cap on the labour rate you will cover (e.g., £60 per hour)-”
  • “What happens if the engine fails and damages other components - are those covered-”
  • “Do I have to use your preferred garage for the refit, or can I choose my own-”
  • “What records do I need to keep (service history, oil change receipts)-”
  • “Who do I contact if I need to make a claim - and what is their response time-”

After the work is done:

  • Keep the invoice and warranty document somewhere safe.
  • Note the date the warranty starts and ends.
  • Follow the supplier’s maintenance recommendations (e.g., oil change intervals, oil grade).
  • Keep receipts for any servicing or repairs done during the warranty period.
Claim Process

The warranty claim process - what to expect

If your replacement engine develops a problem within the warranty period, here is the typical journey.

1

Stop driving immediately

If you hear knocking, rattling, loss of power, or see warning lights - stop driving to prevent further damage. Driving a failing engine usually voids the warranty.

2

Contact the supplier first

Call or email the supplier who sold you the engine. Explain the symptoms. Do not take the car to another garage without telling the supplier first - they may need to inspect the engine themselves.

3

Provide documentation

The supplier will ask for: the original invoice, warranty document, photos or videos of the issue (if visible), fault codes (if available), and service records (oil changes, etc.).

4

Supplier diagnoses the fault

They may ask you to bring the vehicle to them, or to an approved garage, for inspection. They will determine if the failure is covered by the warranty.

5

Claim approved or declined

Approved: The supplier will arrange a replacement engine and (if parts-and-labour) cover the refit labour. You may need to pay for consumables (oil, coolant, gaskets).
Declined: The supplier will explain why (e.g., installation error, neglect, excluded part). You can appeal if you disagree.

6

Escalate if necessary

If the supplier refuses a valid claim or becomes unresponsive, you may have legal rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. For disputes about the supplier’s warranty, Engines Market does not mediate - but we do track complaints as part of our supplier monitoring.

Summary

What you need to remember

Do

  • Get the warranty terms in writing before you pay
  • Ask about exclusions - especially gaskets and consequential damage
  • Keep all invoices and service receipts
  • Follow the supplier’s maintenance schedule
  • Contact the supplier first if something goes wrong

Don't

  • Assume a “12-month warranty” includes labour - ask
  • Ignore warning lights or unusual noises - that may void your cover
  • Use the wrong oil grade - it will void almost any warranty
  • Modify or remap the engine - that will also void cover
  • Drive a failing engine - you will make it worse and lose your claim

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does Engines Market provide warranties on engines-
No. Never. The warranty comes from the supplier you choose. We do not sell, fit, or warranty engines.
What is the most common reason a warranty claim is rejected-
Installation error (wrong oil, incorrect torque, damaged sensors) and neglect (ignoring warning lights, overheating) are the top two reasons. Consequential damage (e.g., the failed engine damaging the turbo) is also frequently excluded.
Can I transfer the warranty if I sell the car-
Some suppliers allow transfer, most do not. Ask before you buy.
What if the supplier goes out of business during my warranty period-
Unfortunately, a warranty is only as good as the company behind it. If the supplier ceases trading, you may have no recourse. This is one reason we monitor supplier status and only accept established businesses (1+ year trading minimum).
Where can I go if I have a dispute with a supplier-
First, try to resolve it directly with the supplier. If that fails, you may have rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. Engines Market does not mediate warranty disputes, but we do track complaints to inform our supplier monitoring and removal decisions.
