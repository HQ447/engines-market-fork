Signs of gearbox failure

How to spot a failing gearbox

Catching gearbox issues early can save you thousands - a simple repair may cost £200-£500, while a full replacement could cost £1,000-£5,000+.

⚠️ Slipping (auto)Engine revs but car doesn't accelerate properly - worn clutches, low fluid
🔊 Grinding (manual)Noise when changing gear - worn synchromesh, damaged gears
⏱️ Delayed engagementDelay when selecting drive/reverse - low fluid, worn valve body
🔊 Whining / moaningNoise in neutral or when driving - worn bearings, low fluid
💥 ClunkingNoise when changing gear or engaging drive - worn mounts, damaged internals
💧 Leaking fluidRed/brown fluid under the car - worn seals, cracked casing
🚨 Warning lightsGearbox warning light on dashboard - electronic issue, low fluid
💥 Jarring shifts (auto/DSG)Harsh or jerky gear changes - worn valve body, low fluid, software issue

What to do if you notice symptoms: Check fluid level and for leaks. Have it inspected by a specialist. Don't ignore symptoms - they won't get better. A diagnostic check can identify issues before they become catastrophic.

Before you agree

10 questions to ask your gearbox supplier

1. "What's included in the price-"

Get a full written breakdown. Ask about: gearbox supply, fitting, fluid, clutch (manual), torque converter (auto), mechatronic, and testing.

2. "What warranty do you offer and what does it cover-"

Ask about: warranty length, parts vs parts and labour, exclusions, mileage limit, and how to claim.

3. "Is the clutch/DMF included (manual)-"

If replacing a manual gearbox, ask if the clutch and DMF are included. It's recommended to replace these while the gearbox is out.

4. "Is the torque converter included (auto)-"

For automatic gearboxes, ask if the torque converter is included. Many recon units exclude this.

5. "Is the mechatronic/control unit included (auto/DSG)-"

For automatic and DSG gearboxes, ask if the control unit is included. This is often a significant extra cost.

6. "What fluid is used and who supplies it-"

Gearbox fluid is expensive and must be the correct spec. Ask if it's included and what brand/type is used.

7. "What's the timeline for replacement-"

Get a realistic estimate. Manual FWD typically takes 1-2 days; auto/DSG 4WD takes 2-4 days.

8. "Do you have experience with my gearbox type-"

Some gearboxes require specialist knowledge and tools. Ask about their experience with your specific gearbox.

9. "Can you provide a written quote-"

Get everything in writing before agreeing. The scope of work, cost, timeline, and warranty terms should all be documented.

10. "What happens if additional issues are discovered-"

During fitting, other issues may be discovered (worn mounts, drive shafts, etc.). Ask how these are handled.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about gearbox replacement

How much does gearbox replacement cost in the UK- +
Gearbox replacement costs range from £500 to £6,500+ depending on gearbox type and condition. Manual gearboxes cost £500-£3,000, while automatic transmissions range from £1,000-£5,000. DSG/DCT and CVT gearboxes typically cost £2,000-£6,500+. Most owners pay between £1,500 and £4,000 fitted.
What's the difference between reconditioned and used gearboxes- +
A used gearbox is removed from a donor vehicle and sold "as-is" with limited warranty (1-3 months). A reconditioned gearbox is disassembled, inspected, worn parts replaced, and tested. It comes with 6-12 months warranty and is more reliable. Reconditioned costs 30-50% more than used.
Should I replace the clutch when replacing a manual gearbox- +
Yes - it's strongly recommended. The labour cost to replace the clutch separately is much higher (the gearbox must be removed again). Doing it together saves 4-6 hours of labour (£300-£600). Most specialists recommend this as standard.
How long does gearbox replacement take- +
Typical fitting time is 1-4 days. Manual FWD takes 1-2 days. Auto/DSG 4WD takes 2-4 days. Complex or rare gearboxes may take longer. The timeline depends on vehicle complexity, parts availability, and the specialist's workload.
What's the labour rate for gearbox replacement- +
Labour rates vary by region. Independent specialists charge £65-£160/hour (regional variation). Main dealers charge £90-£220/hour. Most gearbox replacements take 5-15 hours of labour. Independent specialists are typically 40-60% cheaper than main dealers.
Should I use an independent specialist or a main dealer- +
For most customers, an independent transmission specialist offers the best balance of cost and quality. Main dealers are significantly more expensive (40-60% higher) but offer manufacturer training and specialist tools. Use a main dealer if your vehicle is under warranty or you're claiming on insurance.
What's the most reliable gearbox type- +
Traditional automatic (torque converter) is generally the most reliable when maintained. Manual gearboxes are also very reliable if driven sympathetically. DSG/DCT and CVT are reliable but require regular maintenance (fluid changes) and can be expensive to repair when they fail.
How do I know if my gearbox needs replacing or repairing- +
If the issue is minor (low fluid, sensor fault, software issue), repair may be possible. If there's internal damage (worn clutches, damaged gears, failed bearings), replacement is likely needed. A specialist inspection and diagnostic check will determine the best course of action.
What's the most common gearbox type in the UK- +
Manual gearboxes remain the most common in the UK, though automatic and DSG/DCT are increasingly popular. The most common gearbox replacements we see are: VW Group DSG (DQ250, DQ381), BMW ZF 6HP and 8HP, Mercedes 7G-Tronic, and manual gearboxes in Fiesta, Focus, Golf, and Corsa.
Do I need to tell my insurer about a replacement gearbox- +
Yes. Any major mechanical change, including gearbox replacement, must be declared to your insurer. The new gearbox type and number should be recorded on your insurance policy. Failure to do so could invalidate your insurance.
What's the most common gearbox failure- +
In manual gearboxes: worn synchromesh (grinding when changing gear), worn bearings (whining noise), and clutch failure. In automatic/DSG: mechatronic/control unit failure, worn clutch packs, low fluid, and torque converter failure. In CVT: steel belt failure and valve body issues.
Should I rebuild or replace my gearbox- +
If the gearbox has minor wear, rebuilding may be viable and cheaper. If there's significant internal damage, replacement is usually more cost-effective. A specialist will inspect and advise. Rebuilding typically costs 30-50% less than replacement but isn't suitable for all failures.
What's the difference between DSG and automatic- +
DSG/DCT is a dual-clutch transmission - it's essentially two manual gearboxes combined with computer-controlled clutches. It shifts very quickly and is used in performance cars. Traditional automatic uses a torque converter and planetary gears. DSG is more complex and more expensive to repair/replace.
How often should I change gearbox fluid- +
Manual gearbox fluid: every 60,000-80,000 miles. Automatic/DSG fluid: every 40,000-60,000 miles (or as specified by the manufacturer). CVT fluid: every 30,000-40,000 miles. Regular fluid changes prevent failures and extend gearbox life.
