Rebuilt engine cost summary

What does a rebuilt engine cost in the UK-

The short answer: A rebuilt engine typically costs between £2,500 and £6,000 for a standard 4 or 6-cylinder engine, rising to £8,000-£15,000+ for performance, prestige, or classic car engines.

The longer answer: For most UK drivers rebuilding a common 4-cylinder engine, expect to pay between £2,500 and £4,500 for a full rebuild with machining. Performance 6-cylinder rebuilds typically cost £4,000-£8,000, while V8, V12, or classic car engine rebuilds can reach £8,000-£15,000+ ex VAT.

Based on 24,650 real quote requests processed through Engines Market in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], rebuilt engines represent approximately 1-3% of total replacement requests - but the picture is more nuanced because many suppliers use "reconditioned" and "rebuilt" interchangeably.

Reconditioned vs rebuilt

The industry confusion explained

Here's the most important thing to understand about rebuilt engines in the UK market: many suppliers use "reconditioned" and "rebuilt" interchangeably.

In practice, what one supplier calls a "reconditioned engine" might involve full machining and upgraded components, while another supplier's "rebuilt engine" might be little more than a clean and basic parts replacement. There is no legal definition that separates these terms in the UK engine industry.

What to ask your supplier

1. "Will you machine the block, crank, and head-" - If no, it's a reconditioning job, not a rebuild.
2. "What components will be replaced and with what quality parts-" - Look for forged pistons, uprated bearings, and premium gaskets.
3. "What warranty do you offer and what does it cover-" - 12-24 months indicates a proper rebuild.
4. "Will you provide a full written specification of the rebuild-" - A reputable rebuilder will document everything.

Legal note: We cannot legally say that reconditioned and rebuilt are the same thing because the industry has no standardised definition. What we can say is that in practice, suppliers use the terms synonymously, and the only way to know what you're getting is to ask for a detailed specification in writing.

What "rebuilt" actually includes

The full rebuild process explained

A proper engine rebuild is a comprehensive process that returns your engine to factory (or better) specification.

01
Full Disassembly
Complete strip-down to individual components. Cylinder head, crank, pistons, timing components all removed.
02
Inspection & Measurement
Every component inspected for wear, damage, cracking. Bores measured for ovality and taper.
03
Machining
Block bored/honed, crank reground, head skimmed - the critical difference between rebuild and reconditioning.
04
Parts Replacement
New pistons, bearings, timing components, oil pump, gaskets - all wear items replaced.
05
Assembly
Assembled with precise torque settings, dial gauges, micrometers to verify tolerances.
06
Testing
Compression test, oil pressure test, leak-down test - and optional dyno testing.

Core Exchange vs Send Your Engine

Core Exchange
You send your old engine as a "core" and receive a rebuilt engine that's already been prepared. Your old engine is then rebuilt for the next customer.
⏱️ Timeline: 5-10 days 💰 Cost: 10-15% less ✅ Pros: Faster, cheaper ❌ Cons: Lose matching numbers
Send Your Engine
You remove your engine and send it to the rebuilder. They rebuild your specific engine and return it to you.
⏱️ Timeline: 3-6 weeks 💰 Cost: Full rebuild cost ✅ Pros: Keep matching numbers ❌ Cons: Longer, more expensive
When rebuilt makes sense

Is a rebuilt engine right for you-

A rebuilt engine is a significant investment. Here's when it makes sense.

💎
Premium vehicles (£15,000+)
If your vehicle is worth £15,000 or more, a rebuilt engine is the logical choice for long-term ownership. The investment is proportionate to the vehicle's value.
📅
Keeping the car 3+ years
If you plan to keep your vehicle for 3+ years, a rebuilt engine is the most cost-effective option. The warranty covers the first 1-2 years, and the quality ensures reliability.
🏁
Enthusiasts and modified cars
If you drive hard or have modified your car, a rebuilt engine with upgraded components is essential. Standard reconditioned engines won't handle increased power.
🚗
Classic cars
For classic cars, a rebuilt engine is the only acceptable option for preserving value and originality. Matching numbers and period-correct restoration are essential.
❤️
Sentimental value
Sometimes the decision isn't financial. If the car has sentimental value, a rebuilt engine is the right choice regardless of market value.

✅ What the warranty covers

  • Machining defects: If the block, crank, or head was incorrectly machined
  • Assembly defects: If the engine was incorrectly assembled (incorrect torque, timing, etc.)
  • Parts defects: If any new components fail
  • Labour: The cost of repair if the engine fails under warranty

If you or someone else installed the engine incorrectly

After the warranty period, normal wear is expected

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about rebuilt engines

What's the difference between rebuilt and reconditioned engines- +
A rebuilt engine involves full disassembly, machining of the block/head/crank, and assembly with new parts including upgraded components where specified. Reconditioned engines are cleaned and have worn parts replaced but typically don't include machining or upgraded components. Warranty is also longer on rebuilt engines (12-24 months vs 6-12 months). However, in practice, many suppliers use the terms interchangeably - always ask for a written specification.
How much does a rebuilt engine cost in the UK- +
Rebuilt engine costs range from £2,500 to £6,000 for standard 4 or 6-cylinder engines, rising to £8,000-£15,000+ for performance, prestige, or classic car engines. Most owners pay between £3,000 and £5,000 for a full rebuild. Prices are ex VAT and assume you're sending your engine for rebuild (not a core exchange).
Is a rebuilt engine as good as new- +
A properly rebuilt engine is often better than new because you can specify upgraded components (forged pistons, uprated bearings, performance parts). The machining and assembly should meet or exceed factory tolerances. However, the quality depends entirely on the specialist doing the work - choose carefully.
How long does a rebuilt engine last- +
A properly rebuilt engine should last 100,000+ miles if maintained correctly. The rebuild includes new pistons, rings, bearings, timing components, and gaskets - essentially every wear item is replaced. Regular oil changes and servicing are essential for longevity.
Should I rebuild or replace my engine- +
If your vehicle is worth £15,000+ and you plan to keep it for 3+ years, a rebuild is the best choice. If the vehicle is worth under £5,000, consider a used or reconditioned engine. If the vehicle is a classic or has sentimental value, a rebuild is the only option.
What warranty comes with a rebuilt engine- +
Standard rebuilds typically come with 12-18 months warranty. Performance rebuilds often offer 18-24 months. Concours/prestige rebuilds can offer 24+ months. The warranty typically covers machining defects, assembly defects, and parts failures. Customer-induced damage is excluded.
Can I upgrade components during a rebuild- +
Yes, absolutely. This is one of the main advantages of a rebuild. You can specify forged pistons, uprated bearings, performance camshafts, upgraded valve springs, and more. Discuss your goals with the rebuilder - they can recommend appropriate upgrades.
What's the difference between core exchange and sending my engine- +
Core exchange: You send your old engine and receive a rebuilt engine that's already prepared. Faster (5-10 days), cheaper (10-15% less), but you lose your original engine (matching numbers). Send your engine: Your specific engine is rebuilt. Slower (3-6 weeks), more expensive, but you keep your original engine (matching numbers preserved).
How long does a rebuild take- +
Standard 4-cylinder rebuild: 2-3 weeks. 6-cylinder rebuild: 3-4 weeks. V8/V12 or classic engine rebuild: 4-8 weeks. Timeline depends on machine shop workload and parts availability. Core exchange is faster (5-10 days).
Do I need to tell my insurer about a rebuilt engine- +
Yes. Any major mechanical change, including a rebuilt engine, must be declared to your insurer. The new engine specification and number should be recorded on your V5C (logbook) and insurance policy. Failure to do so could invalidate your insurance.
What's the most popular engine for rebuilds- +
Based on our data, the most common engines rebuilt are: BMW N47 (timing chain), VW/Audi EA189 2.0 TDI (DPF/EGR issues), Land Rover TDV6/SDV6 (crankshaft failure), Mercedes OM642 (oil cooler seal), and Jaguar AJ200D Ingenium (timing chain). Classic engine rebuilds are common for Jaguar XK, MGB, and Mini.
What hidden costs should I watch out for- +
Common hidden costs in a rebuild: ancillary parts (alternator, starter, turbo) that fail after the rebuild - you'll pay labour again to replace them. Shipping costs for engine transport. Dyno testing (often optional and extra). Collection and delivery of the engine. VAT (most prices are ex VAT).
Can I rebuild a high-mileage engine- +
Yes, but the cost-effectiveness depends on the engine's condition. If the block and crank are in good condition (no cracks, minimal wear), a rebuild is viable. If the engine is severely worn or damaged, replacement may be cheaper. The rebuilder will inspect and advise.
Is a rebuilt engine covered by the Consumer Rights Act- +
If you buy a rebuilt engine as a consumer (not a business), the Consumer Rights Act 2015 applies. The engine must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, and as described. If it fails within 30 days, you can reject it. Within 6 months, the supplier must prove it wasn't faulty at the time of delivery. Warranty terms (12-24 months) should be in writing.
