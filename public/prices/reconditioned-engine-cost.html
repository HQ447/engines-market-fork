Reconditioned engine cost summary

What does a reconditioned engine cost in the UK-

The short answer: Reconditioned engines cost between £2,100 and £3,500 supply-only for most 4-cylinder engines. Fitted costs range from £3,500 to £5,500 depending on engine type, vehicle, and location.

The longer answer: For most UK drivers replacing a common 4-cylinder diesel or petrol engine, expect to pay £2,100-£3,500 supply-only for a reconditioned unit with a 12-month warranty. Fitting adds £800-£2,200 depending on your location and the garage you choose.

Why reconditioned is the most popular choice: Based on 24,650 quote requests in 2025, reconditioned engines were chosen in 70.45% of cases [EM-VERIFIED]. For vehicles worth between £4,000 and £15,000, reconditioned was the most common choice, reflecting the balance of cost and warranty.

Understanding reconditioned engines

What does "reconditioned" actually mean-

Understanding what a reconditioned engine actually is - and what it isn't - is critical before you buy one. Unlike a used engine, which is sold "as-is," a reconditioned engine has been professionally rebuilt to a defined standard.

The 7-Step Reconditioning Process

This is the typical process used by reputable UK engine specialists. Quality varies between suppliers, so understanding the process helps you ask the right questions.

01
Strip Down
Complete disassembly. Every component removed for inspection.
02
Clean and Degrease
Hot wash or shot-blast to remove carbon, oil, dirt, and corrosion.
03
Inspect and Measure
Every component inspected for wear, damage, or cracking. Bores measured for ovality.
04
Machining
Cylinder bores honed, crankshaft journals reground, head skimmed if warped.
05
Replace Worn Components
Piston rings, bearings, seals, timing components, oil pump all replaced.
06
Rebuild and Assemble
Reassembled to manufacturer specs with new wearing parts.
07
Test and Run
Compression test, oil pressure test, leak-down test, running test.

What components are typically replaced

✅ Piston rings
✅ Main bearings
✅ Big end bearings
✅ Thrust washers
✅ Timing chain / belt
✅ Timing guides and tensioners
✅ Oil pump (usually)
✅ Water pump (usually)
✅ Valve stem seals
✅ Gaskets and seals (all new)
✅ Freeze plugs (usually)
✅ Oil filter housing (sometimes)

Reconditioned engine warranty

What's covered - and what's not

The warranty is the biggest reason to choose a reconditioned engine over a used one. Unlike used engines (which are often parts-only), reconditioned engines typically come with a comprehensive warranty covering both parts and labour.

Typical Warranty Duration

DurationTypical CoverageCommon For
6 monthsParts + LabourBudget reconditioned engines
12 monthsParts + LabourMost reconditioned engines (standard)
18 monthsParts + LabourPremium reconditioned engines
24 monthsParts + LabourHigh-end reconditioned / rebuilt engines

Most common: 12 months parts and labour is the standard for reconditioned engines from reputable suppliers.

✅ What Warranty Typically Covers

  • Internal engine components: Pistons, rings, bearings, camshaft, crankshaft, connecting rods
  • Cylinder head components: Valves, springs, camshafts (if included)
  • Structural integrity: Block cracks (if discovered early)
  • Timing components: If replaced as part of the reconditioning
  • Labour: To remove and refit the engine if it fails within the warranty period

❌ What Warranty Typically Does NOT Cover

🔧
Incorrect installation
If the engine is fitted incorrectly, the warranty is void. Always use a qualified mechanic.
⚠️
Neglect
Running with no oil, overheating, or ignoring warning lights voids the warranty.
🏁
Racing or track use
Reconditioned engines are not warranted for performance use.
⚙️
Ancillary components
Alternator, starter, injectors, turbo, pumps are usually not covered unless explicitly stated.
🔍
Unforeseen damage
If fitting reveals additional issues (damaged wiring, broken mounts), these are not covered.
🔩
Wear and tear
Normal wear components (seals, gaskets, belts) are not covered.
🚗
Vehicle modification
If the engine is fitted to a modified vehicle, the warranty may be void.

The Labour Coverage Advantage

This is the single biggest advantage of a reconditioned engine over a used engine.

Scenario (Used Engine): You buy a used engine for £1,200 with a 3-month parts-only warranty. After 6 weeks, it fails. The supplier replaces the engine, but you pay £800-£1,500 labour again.

Scenario (Reconditioned Engine): You buy a reconditioned engine for £2,800 with a 12-month parts and labour warranty. After 6 months, it fails. The supplier replaces the engine AND covers the labour to remove and refit it.

Result: The reconditioned engine costs more upfront but carries significantly less financial risk.

How to choose a reconditioned engine supplier

8-point supplier checklist

Not all reconditioned engines are created equal. Here's how to choose a reputable supplier.

1. Reconditioning process

Ask for a detailed description of what's replaced and tested

2. Warranty terms

12 months minimum, parts AND labour

3. Evidence of testing

Compression test report, video of engine running

4. Parts list

What components were replaced (timing, bearings, seals, etc.)

5. Photos

Ask for photos of the actual engine, not stock images

6. Reviews

Check Google, Trustpilot, Facebook reviews

7. Business registration

UK registered company with physical address

8. Trading history

At least 12-24 months in business

10 Questions to Ask Your Supplier

1. "What does your reconditioning process include-" - Ask for details on strip, clean, inspect, machine, replace, rebuild, test.
2. "What parts are replaced as standard-" - Ask specifically about timing components, bearings, seals, and gaskets.
3. "What's the warranty and what does it cover-" - Get it in writing. Confirm labour is covered.
4. "Can you provide a compression test report-" - A reputable supplier will have this.
5. "Can you send a video of the engine running-" - This is the best evidence of quality.
6. "Do you have photos of the actual engine-" - Not stock photos. The exact engine you're buying.
7. "Who fits the engine and what warranty do they offer-" - If the supplier doesn't fit, ask about the fitter's qualifications.
8. "What's the delivery and returns policy-" - Who pays if the engine fails under warranty-
9. "Do you have examples of this engine installed in the same vehicle-" - Experience with your specific model matters.
10. "Can you provide a written quote with all costs itemised-" - Avoid surprises.

Yes, for most applications. A reconditioned engine has been stripped, cleaned, inspected, and rebuilt with new wearing parts. It includes a 12-month parts and labour warranty. A used engine is sold "as-is" with unknown history and minimal warranty.
What's the difference between reconditioned and rebuilt- +
Reconditioned engines are rebuilt to a defined standard with new wearing parts and full testing. Rebuilt engines vary in quality - some are full rebuilds with upgraded components, others are basic repairs. Always ask what's included.
What warranty comes with a reconditioned engine- +
Typically 12 months parts and labour. Some premium suppliers offer 18 or 24 months. Always confirm in writing.
Is a reconditioned engine worth the extra cost over used- +
For most vehicles worth £4,000-£15,000, yes. The £600-£1,000 premium buys a 12-month parts and labour warranty, new wearing parts, and peace of mind. If your vehicle is worth under £4,000, a used engine may be more cost-effective.
How long does a reconditioned engine last- +
A properly reconditioned engine should last 80,000-150,000 miles with proper maintenance. The quality of the reconditioning and your maintenance after installation are the key factors.
What's included in a reconditioned engine- +
Typically: new piston rings, bearings, gaskets, seals, timing components (where applicable), and sometimes oil pump and water pump. The engine is fully tested before delivery.
Can I fit a reconditioned engine myself- +
Only if you're a qualified mechanic. Most warranties require professional installation by a recognised garage. DIY installation will void the warranty.
How do I know if my vehicle needs a reconditioned engine- +
If your engine has suffered catastrophic failure (snapped timing belt on interference engine, rod knock, crankshaft failure, severe overheating) and your vehicle is worth over £4,000, a reconditioned engine is usually the best option.

How much does fitting a reconditioned engine cost- +
Fitting typically costs £800-£2,200 depending on engine type and labour rates. 4-cylinder engines: 6-11 hours. 6-cylinder: 10-14 hours. V8: 14-20+ hours. Regional labour rates vary from £65/hr (North) to £180/hr (London).
Is a reconditioned engine cheaper than a new engine- +
Yes. A reconditioned engine typically costs 40-70% less than a new OEM engine. For a typical 4-cylinder, new costs £4,500-£8,000+ while reconditioned costs £2,100-£3,500.
