Each condition explained

Which one is right for your car-

Used engine
£1,500-£2,800
Direct from a donor vehicle. Mileage known but history often unverified. Budget option for older cars. Short warranty (1-6 months).
Reconditioned engine
£2,100-£3,500
Disassembled, cleaned, worn parts replaced (pistons, rings, bearings, seals). Tested to standard. Best value for most owners. 70.45% of all requests [EM-VERIFIED].
Rebuilt engine
£3,000-£5,000
Full strip and rebuild with upgraded components (forged pistons, improved oil pumps). For long-term ownership or enthusiast vehicles.

Factory-spec rebuild using all new parts (pistons, liners, bearings, timing components). Often includes BS AU257 certification. Premium.
New OEM engine

Brand new from manufacturer. Highest cost; generally only for insurance/warranty or collector cars. 12-24 months warranty.

What's included - and what's not

Know exactly what you're paying for

Clarity matters. Below is a typical breakdown for a reconditioned engine fitted by an independent specialist.

✅ Typically included

🔧
Engine supply
Complete reconditioned long block or short block, as quoted.
🛠️
Labour to remove and refit
8-15 hours at agreed rate. Includes fluid drain and refill.
🔄
Disposal of old unit
Old engine taken away and scrapped (environmentally compliant).
🔁
Consumables
New oil, filter, coolant, basic gaskets. Sometimes timing belt/chain if part of the rebuild.
📋
Basic ancillaries transfer
Moving your existing alternator, starter, etc. to the new engine.
🛡️
Warranty
6-12 months on reconditioned engines (parts and labour).

⚠️ Typically NOT included (extra cost)

🔗
Clutch / DMF
Manual gearbox: clutch and dual-mass flywheel often worn. Extra £400-£1,000.
💧
Water pump / thermostat
Often recommended as a precaution. Add £100-£300 if not included.
❄️
Air-con regas
Removing engine requires disconnecting air-con pipes. Regas extra £50-£150.
🔧
Timing belt / chain
Some reconditioned engines supplied without. Add £300-£800 if not included.
🛡️
Ancillary parts
Alternator, starter, injectors, turbo may be worn. £200-£1,000+ if they fail.
🔍
Unforeseen repairs
Worn mounts, exhaust leaks, corroded pipes discovered during fitting. Quoted separately.
📦
Delivery & pallet return
Delivery £50-£200. Pallet return £50-£100 if required.
🚗
Hire / courtesy car
Unless offered, you'll need alternative transport during the 5.2-day average timeline.

Always ask your supplier for a full written breakdown before agreeing to any work. Additional costs are the biggest source of disputes.

Is replacement worth it-

Weighing cost against vehicle value

Some owners consider whether a new engine makes financial sense. Here's a general guide based on market values.

Under £2,000Consider scrapping - replacement cost may exceed the car's value.
£2,000 - £4,000Used engine may be viable if the car is otherwise excellent. Reconditioned rarely cost-effective.
£4,000 - £8,000Evaluate both used and reconditioned. Reconditioned may be viable for long-term ownership.
£8,000 - £15,000Reconditioned is the most popular choice - warranty and reliability justify the premium.
£15,000+Reconditioned or rebuilt is well within value. Replacement is a sound investment.

If the lowest viable replacement cost exceeds approximately 60% of the vehicle's current market value, evaluate scrap/part-ex value before committing.

Before you agree

10 questions to ask your engine supplier

Asking the right questions can save you thousands and prevent disputes. Here's a checklist.

1. What exactly is included-

Get a full written breakdown. Ask about delivery, pallet return, disposal, and consumables.

2. What is NOT included-

Ask about clutch/DMF, water pump, timing components, ancillaries, and unforeseen repairs.

3. What warranty do you offer-

Get it in writing. Check if parts only or parts + labour. Ask about the claims process.

4. What is the engine's history-

For used: mileage, vehicle source, known issues. For recon: what parts were replaced-

5. Do you have photos of the actual engine-

Ask for photos of the specific engine you're buying, not a stock image.

6. Who will do the fitting-

Ask for the garage name, address, and experience with your engine type.

7. What's the expected timeline-

Get a realistic estimate from deposit to completion. Ask about courtesy car options.

8. What happens if something goes wrong-

Ask about complaints process, warranty claims, and dispute resolution.

9. Can I see your reviews-

Check Google, Trustpilot, and Facebook reviews independently.

10. Do you have a UK business address-

Verify the supplier is a legitimate UK business with Companies House registration.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about engine replacement costs

How much does engine replacement cost in the UK- +
Engine replacement costs range from £1,700 to £7,350+ depending on vehicle type and condition. Most owners pay between £2,500 and £4,500 for a fitted reconditioned engine. Supply-only costs start from £1,500 for used and £2,100 for reconditioned.
What's the difference between used, reconditioned, rebuilt, and new- +
Used: from a donor vehicle, unknown history. Reconditioned: cleaned, worn parts replaced, tested. Rebuilt: stripped and rebuilt with a mixture of new and serviceable parts. Remanufactured: factory-spec rebuild with strict tolerances. New: brand new from manufacturer. See the table above for costs and best-use cases.
Is it cheaper to repair or replace an engine- +
If damage is isolated (timing chain rattle caught early, head gasket seepage), targeted repair may be cheaper. Advanced failure (rod knock, snapped timing belt on interference engine) often makes replacement more cost-effective. → Repair vs Replacement comparison
Does engine replacement cost include fitting- +
Not always. Always confirm with your supplier. Our price tables show supply-only and fitted costs separately. Fitting typically adds £800-£2,200 depending on engine type and labour rates.
How long does an engine replacement take- +
From quote request to completion, the average is 5.2 days [EM-VERIFIED]. Fitting labour typically takes 1-3 days depending on engine complexity. Allow 2-5 days for engine sourcing and delivery.
What warranty comes with a replacement engine- +
Used engines: 1-6 months. Reconditioned: 6-12 months. Rebuilt and remanufactured: 12-24 months. New OEM: 12-24 months. Engines Market does not provide warranties - we connect you with suppliers who do. Always check the supplier's warranty terms in writing.
Can I fit a used engine from a different vehicle- +
Yes, but you need the exact same engine code and often the same variant to ensure compatibility. Your supplier will match the engine code to your vehicle's VIN. Never assume a similar engine will fit.
What happens to my old engine- +
Your old engine is typically collected and scrapped by the supplier or garage. This is usually included in the fitting cost. Some suppliers may offer a discount if your old engine has salvageable parts (e.g., cylinder head, turbo, injectors).
Should I replace the clutch/DMF at the same time- +
If you have a manual gearbox and the engine is out, absolutely consider it. The labour cost to replace a clutch/DMF is much higher if done separately. Many owners regret not doing it "while the engine is out."
Can I drive with a failing engine- +
It depends. If you hear a deep knock, see the oil pressure warning light, or notice significant power loss - stop driving immediately. Continuing will cause more damage and increase the repair cost.
How do I know if my car is worth repairing- +
A rough rule: if the replacement cost exceeds 60% of your vehicle's market value, consider scrapping. However, rare, collectable, or sentimental vehicles may justify spending above this threshold.
What hidden costs should I watch out for- +
Common hidden costs: clutch/DMF, water pump, timing belt/chain, air-con regas, delivery charges, pallet return fees, and unforeseen repairs discovered during fitting. Always ask for a written breakdown.
Is engine replacement covered by car insurance- +
Only if you have a comprehensive policy and the damage is from an insured event (e.g., accident, fire, theft). Mechanical failure is not usually covered. However, if you have a third-party or used car warranty, engine failure may be covered - check your policy.
Do I need to tell my insurer about an engine replacement- +
Yes. Any major mechanical change, including an engine replacement, must be declared to your insurer. Failure to do so could invalidate your insurance. The new engine code and number should be recorded on your V5C (logbook) and insurance policy.
