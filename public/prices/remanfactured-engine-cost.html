Remanufactured engine cost summary

What does a remanufactured engine cost in the UK-

The short answer: A remanufactured engine typically costs £3,000-£7,000+ depending on engine size and application. This is 40% less than a new OE unit while delivering equivalent performance and reliability. Fitting adds £800-£2,200 depending on engine type and region.

The longer answer: For most UK drivers replacing a common 4-cylinder engine with a remanufactured unit, expect to pay between £3,000 and £5,000 for the engine supply. Premium 6-cylinder and V8 remanufactured engines typically cost £5,000-£7,000+. Commercial vehicle remanufactured engines for vans and light trucks range from £4,000-£8,000+.

Based on 24,650 real quote requests processed through Engines Market in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], remanufactured engines represent approximately 2-3% of total replacement requests - but this is growing as fleets and insurance companies recognise the value proposition.

BS AU257:2002 Code of Practice

BS AU257:2002 Code of Practice for the Remanufacture of Automotive Engines
  • Provide performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine
  • Be returned to manufacturer's specification
  • Use new parts for all critical components
  • Pass rigorous testing to verify performance
  • Be supplied with a comprehensive warranty

[THIRD-PARTY] British Standards Institution

Important: BS AU257:2002 is the industry benchmark for quality. Any engine described as "remanufactured" should meet or exceed this standard. Always ask your supplier if they are BS AU257:2002-certified.

Core exchange vs sending your engine

Two ways to get a remanufactured engine

Most remanufacturers offer core exchange as the default option. Here's how it works.

🔄 Core Exchange (Recommended)
You send your old engine as a "core" and receive a remanufactured engine that's already been prepared. Your old engine is then remanufactured for the next customer.
⏱️ Timeline: 5-10 days 💰 Cost: 10-15% less ✅ Pros: Faster, cheaper, consistent quality ❌ Cons: Lose matching numbers
Best for: Commercial fleets, insurance jobs, daily drivers
📦 Send Your Engine
You remove your engine and send it to the remanufacturer. They remanufacture your specific engine and return it to you.
⏱️ Timeline: 3-6 weeks 💰 Cost: Full remanufacturing cost ✅ Pros: Keep matching numbers ❌ Cons: Longer, more expensive
Best for: Classic cars, matching-numbers restorations

Remanufactured engine warranty

What's included

The industry standard for remanufactured engines is a 12-month unlimited mileage parts and labour warranty. This is significantly better than reconditioned engines (6-12 months, often mileage-limited) and equivalent to many new OEM engines.

✅ What the warranty covers

  • Parts: All replacement components (pistons, rings, bearings, timing components, gaskets, seals)
  • Labour: The cost of repair if the engine fails under warranty
  • Machining defects: If the block, crank, or head was incorrectly machined
  • Assembly defects: If the engine was incorrectly assembled
  • Unlimited mileage: No mileage limit during the 12-month period

❌ What the warranty typically excludes

⚠️
Customer-induced damage
Running without oil, overheating, over-revving
⚙️
Ancillary failures
Alternator, starter, turbo, injectors (unless part of remanufacture)
🔧
Installation errors
If you or someone else installed the engine incorrectly
🔩
Normal wear and tear
After the 12-month period, normal wear is expected

Before you agree

10 questions to ask your remanufacturer

1. "Are you BS AU257:2002 certified-"

This is the single most important question. If they aren't certified, they shouldn't call their engines "remanufactured."

2. "What's included in the remanufacturing process-"

Get a full written breakdown. Ask about machining, parts replacement, testing, and documentation.

3. "What warranty do you offer and what does it cover-"

Industry standard is 12-month unlimited mileage parts and labour. Get it in writing.

4. "Do you offer core exchange or do I send my engine-"

Most offer core exchange. Ask about the process, timeline, and any discount for providing a suitable core.

5. "Can you provide BS AU257:2002 certification documentation-"

A reputable remanufacturer will provide documentation showing they meet the standard. Ask to see it before agreeing.

6. "What new parts are used in the remanufacture-"

All critical components should be replaced with new parts. Ask about pistons, rings, bearings, timing components, gaskets, and seals.

7. "What testing is performed-"

Comprehensive testing should include compression, oil pressure, leak-down, and often dynamic testing. Ask for a summary of test results.

8. "Can I see photos of previous remanufactured engines-"

Look for attention to detail and cleanliness.

9. "What's the expected timeline from order to delivery-"

Core exchange is typically 5-10 days. Sending your engine is 3-6 weeks. Ask about factors that could delay the timeline.

10. "Can I see your reviews-"

Check Google, Trustpilot, and Facebook reviews independently. Look for reviews from customers with similar vehicles.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about remanufactured engines

What's the difference between remanufactured and reconditioned engines- +
A remanufactured engine is returned to manufacturer's specification with new parts throughout and must meet BS AU257:2002 Code of Practice. It provides performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine. A reconditioned engine is cleaned, has worn parts replaced, but doesn't necessarily meet any standard and often doesn't include full machining or new parts throughout. Remanufactured engines cost 30-40% more but offer significantly higher quality and reliability.
How much does a remanufactured engine cost in the UK- +
Remanufactured engine costs range from £3,000 to £7,000+ depending on engine size and application. Small 4-cylinder engines cost £3,000-£4,000; 6-cylinder engines cost £4,500-£6,500; V8 and commercial engines cost £5,500-£8,500+. Fitting adds £800-£2,200. Prices are ex VAT and assume core exchange.
Is a remanufactured engine as good as new- +
Yes. BS AU257:2002 requires that a remanufactured engine provides performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine. With new parts throughout and comprehensive testing, a remanufactured engine delivers like-new performance at 40% less than a new OE unit.
What's BS AU257:2002- +
BS AU257:2002 is the British Standard Code of Practice for the Remanufacture of Automotive Engines. It specifies that a remanufactured engine must provide performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine, be returned to manufacturer's specification, and be supplied with a comprehensive warranty. Always ask your supplier if they are BS AU257:2002-certified.
Why is remanufactured cheaper than new- +
Remanufactured engines cost 40% less than new OE units because: the engine block, crankshaft, and cylinder head are remanufactured rather than newly cast; remanufacturers process hundreds of the same engine type (economies of scale); there's no manufacturer markup; and remanufacturers have efficient, specialised processes.
What warranty comes with a remanufactured engine- +
Industry standard is a 12-month unlimited mileage parts and labour warranty. Some remanufacturers offer extended warranties (24 months) for an additional cost. The warranty covers machining defects, assembly defects, and parts failures. Customer-induced damage is excluded.
How long does a remanufactured engine last- +
A properly remanufactured engine meeting BS AU257:2002 should last 100,000+ miles if maintained correctly. The remanufacture includes new pistons, rings, bearings, timing components, and gaskets - essentially every wear item is replaced. Regular oil changes and servicing are essential for longevity.
What's core exchange- +
Core exchange is where you send your old engine as a "core" and receive a remanufactured engine that's already been prepared. It's faster (5-10 days), cheaper (10-15% discount), but you lose your original engine (matching numbers). It's the most common option for fleets and insurance jobs.
Can I keep my original engine (matching numbers)- +
Yes, but you'll need to use the "send your engine" option. Your engine is remanufactured and returned to you. This takes longer (3-6 weeks), costs more (no core discount), but preserves matching numbers. It's essential for classic cars and high-value vehicles.
Who should choose a remanufactured engine- +
Commercial fleets (minimising downtime, cost-effective), classic car owners (matching original spec, value preservation), insurance/warranty claims (documented quality, like-new performance), and private owners with £25,000+ vehicles who plan to keep the car 5+ years.
What's the most popular engine for remanufacturing- +
Based on our data: Ford Transit 2.0 EcoBlue (fleet demand), BMW N47/N57 (timing chain issues), Land Rover TDV6/SDV6 (crankshaft bearing failure), Mercedes OM651/OM642 (oil cooler seal), and Volkswagen/Audi 2.0 TDI (DPF/EGR issues). Classic car remanufacturing is also significant for Jaguar E-Type, MGB, and Mini.
Do I need to tell my insurer about a remanufactured engine- +
Yes. Any major mechanical change, including a remanufactured engine, must be declared to your insurer. The new engine specification and number should be recorded on your V5C (logbook) and insurance policy. Failure to do so could invalidate your insurance.
Is a remanufactured engine environmentally friendly- +
Yes. Remanufacturing uses significantly less energy and resources than manufacturing a new engine. The engine block, crankshaft, and cylinder head are reused rather than being recycled or scrapped. This reduces waste and carbon emissions. It's a circular economy approach.
Can I upgrade components during remanufacturing- +
Yes, but remanufacturing is typically about returning to original specification. If you want upgraded components (forged pistons, uprated bearings, performance parts), you may be better off with a performance rebuild. Discuss your requirements with the remanufacturer - they may accommodate modest upgrades.
