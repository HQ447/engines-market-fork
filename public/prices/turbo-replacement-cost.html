Turbo replacement cost summary

What does turbo replacement cost in the UK-

The short answer: Turbo replacement costs range from £450 to £3,000+ depending on single vs twin turbo, vehicle make, and parts quality. Small cars typically cost £450-£1,500, family cars £800-£2,500, and premium/performance vehicles £1,500-£3,500+. Repair services start from £195+VAT, exchange remanufactured units from £495+VAT, and new turbos from £1,000+.

Single turbo vs twin turbo
🔄 Single Turbo
One turbocharger. Typical cost £450-£2,500. Labour 3-8 hours. Common on most 4-cylinder diesels and petrols.
⚡ Twin Turbo
Two turbos. Typical cost £1,500-£5,000+. Labour 6-15 hours. Common on V6, V8, and performance vehicles.

Twin turbo costs more due to double the parts, more labour, and greater complexity.

Signs of turbo failure

How to spot a failing turbocharger

📉 Loss of powerTurbo isn't producing boost - have it inspected, don't ignore
💨 Excessive smoke (blue/white)Oil passing through turbo seals - immediate inspection required
🖤 Excessive smoke (black)Over-fuelling due to boost issues - have it inspected
🔊 Whistling / siren noiseTurbo bearing wear, boost leak - stop driving if severe
🚨 Engine management lightBoost pressure fault codes - diagnostic check required
🔥 Oil burning smellOil leaking from turbo - immediate inspection required
💥 Grinding noiseTurbo bearing failure - STOP DRIVING immediately
📊 Boost pressure errorUnderboost or overboost codes - diagnostic check required

The ticking clock: A failing turbo is a ticking time bomb. If the turbo fails completely: metal debris enters the oil system → contaminated oil circulates → engine bearings are damaged → catastrophic engine failure (£4,000+ to replace).

Repair vs exchange vs new

Understanding your options

🔧 Turbo Repair
£195-£600+
Turbo is stripped, worn parts replaced (bearings, seals, actuator). Rebalanced and tested. 6-12 months warranty. Best for minor issues - worn bearings, actuator problems.
🔄 Exchange / Remanufactured
£495-£1,500+
Your old turbo as core exchange. Receive fully rebuilt, rebalanced, tested unit. 12-24 months warranty. Best for most owners - best balance of cost and quality.
🆕 New (OEM/Aftermarket)
£500-£3,000+
Brand new turbo from OEM or aftermarket (Garrett, BorgWarner). 12-24 months warranty. Best for insurance claims, high-value vehicles, collectors.

Oil supply and return lines

Why these often need replacing

When a turbo fails, it can send debris into the oil system. The oil supply line (feed) and return line can become blocked with debris.

LineTypical CostWhy Replace
Oil feed line£30-£100Can be blocked with debris - starves new turbo of oil
Oil return line£30-£100Can be blocked with debris - oil can't drain from turbo
Banjo bolts and washers£10-£30Sealing washers are single-use

The risk of not replacing: If the oil feed line is blocked, the new turbo will be starved of oil and will fail prematurely. This is one of the most common reasons for premature turbo failure.

Before you agree

10 questions to ask before replacing your turbo

1. "What's included in the replacement price-"

Get a full written breakdown. Ask about: turbo unit, gaskets, seals, oil and filter change, oil lines, and labour.

2. "Is this a repair, exchange, or new turbo-"

Understand what you're paying for. Repair is cheapest but may not be possible. Exchange is best value. New is most expensive.

3. "What warranty do you offer on the replacement-"

Typical warranty: repair 6-12 months, exchange 12-24 months, new 12-24 months. Get it in writing.

4. "Will you flush the oil system-"

When a turbo fails, it sends debris into the oil system. The oil system should be flushed before the new turbo is fitted.

5. "Will you replace the oil feed and return lines-"

These lines can be blocked with debris. Many specialists recommend replacing them to prevent premature failure.

6. "What's the timeline from booking to completion-"

Small car turbo replacement typically takes 1-2 days. Twin turbo/SUV takes 2-4 days.

7. "Is the intercooler contaminated-"

If the turbo has failed, the intercooler may be contaminated with oil. It may need cleaning or replacement.

8. "Do you have experience with my vehicle/engine type-"

Some turbos require specialist knowledge. Ask about their experience with your specific vehicle.

9. "What's the labour cost and how many hours-"

Get a clear breakdown of labour hours and rates. This should be part of the written quote.

10. "Can you provide a written quote-"

Get everything in writing before agreeing. The scope of work, cost, timeline, and warranty terms should all be documented.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about turbo replacement

How much does turbo replacement cost in the UK- +
Turbo replacement costs range from £450 to £3,000+ depending on single vs twin turbo, vehicle make, and parts quality. Small cars cost £450-£1,500, family cars £800-£2,500, and premium/performance vehicles £1,500-£3,500+. Repair services start from £195+VAT, exchange units from £495+VAT, and new turbos from £1,000+.
What are the signs of a failing turbo- +
Loss of power, excessive smoke (blue/white), whistling or siren noise, engine management light, oil burning smell, grinding noise, and boost pressure errors. Whistling and loss of power are the most common early signs.
Can I drive with a failing turbo- +
No - you should stop driving as soon as possible. A failing turbo can send metal debris into the oil system, damaging the engine. If the turbo seizes completely, it can cause catastrophic engine failure (£4,000+ to replace).
What's the difference between turbo repair, exchange, and new- +
Repair: Turbo stripped and worn parts replaced (£195-£600). Exchange: Your old turbo exchanged for a remanufactured unit (£495-£1,500+). New: Brand new turbo from OEM or aftermarket (£500-£3,000+). Exchange is the most popular option - best balance of cost and quality.
How long does turbo replacement take- +
Typical replacement time is 1-4 days. Small car turbos take 3-5 hours (1-2 days with parts ordering). Twin turbo and SUV turbos take 8-20 hours (2-4 days with parts ordering).
Does turbo replacement require an oil change- +
Yes - absolutely essential. When a turbo fails, it sends metal debris into the oil system. The oil and filter must be changed to prevent the debris from damaging the new turbo or engine bearings.
Should I replace oil lines when replacing a turbo- +
Yes - strongly recommended. The oil feed and return lines can be blocked with debris from the failed turbo. If they're not replaced or cleaned, the new turbo may be starved of oil and fail prematurely.
What happens if I ignore a failing turbo- +
The turbo will eventually fail completely. When it does, metal debris enters the oil system, contaminating the engine oil. This damages engine bearings, camshafts, and other components. Engine replacement costs £4,000+. Early replacement is much cheaper.
Why does turbo replacement cost more on some vehicles- +
Turbo location affects labour time. Turbos at the front are accessible (3-5 hours). Turbos at the rear (some BMWs, Mercedes) require significant disassembly (8-15 hours). Twin turbos cost double the parts cost and more labour.
What's the most common turbo failure- +
The most common failure is worn bearings - caused by oil starvation (low oil, infrequent changes), debris in the oil, or age. Actuator failure is also common, especially on VAG (VW/Audi) vehicles.
Is it worth repairing a turbo or should I replace it- +
If the turbo has minor wear (worn bearings, seal leak), repair is viable and cheaper. If the turbo has seized or has significant damage, exchange or new replacement is the only option. A specialist will inspect and advise.
Can I replace a turbo myself- +
Turbo replacement is not a DIY job for most owners. It requires specialist tools, knowledge of the turbo system, and careful handling to prevent damage. Incorrect installation can cause premature failure. Use a specialist.
How long does a turbo last- +
A turbo should last 100,000-150,000 miles with regular oil changes and proper maintenance. However, some turbos fail earlier due to design issues (common on some engines) or poor maintenance (infrequent oil changes).
Does turbo replacement affect insurance- +
Yes - any major mechanical work, including turbo replacement, should be declared to your insurer. The work may affect vehicle value and should be recorded.
