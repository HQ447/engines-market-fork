Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about local garages vs specialists

Is an engine specialist really worth the extra cost- +
For complex work like timing chains (BMW N47, Land Rover Ingenium), full engine replacements, or diagnostic mysteries, yes. The specialist's higher hourly rate is offset by faster diagnosis, no parts markup, simpler warranty handling, and fewer comebacks. For simple repairs, a local garage is fine.
How much does a local garage charge per hour- +
Local garage labour rates typically range from £60 to £160 per hour, depending on region. The average is around £80-£100/hour in most of the UK, with London and the South East at the higher end.
How much does an engine specialist charge per hour- +
Specialist rates are typically £80-£120/hour, but many specialists offer fixed-fee packages for common jobs (e.g., timing chain, engine replacement). This can provide better cost certainty than time-and-materials billing.
Which is better for an engine replacement: local garage or specialist- +
For engine replacement, a specialist is generally recommended. They have direct supplier relationships, better warranty handling, and more experience with the specific engine. The cost is often comparable, but the specialist offers single-point liability.
Why do specialists charge more but sometimes cost less overall- +
Specialists have brand-specific diagnostic equipment, known failure patterns, and direct supply networks. This means they diagnose faults faster (1 hour vs 4 hours), don't mark up parts (10-20% saving), and have fewer comebacks. The total cost can be lower despite a higher hourly rate.
Can a local garage do a timing chain on a BMW N47- +
Some local garages can, but it's risky. The N47 requires specialist timing tools and knowledge of common failure points. Many local garages lack this experience, leading to longer job times, mistakes, and potential engine damage. A specialist is strongly recommended.
What is the biggest risk of using a local garage for complex engine work- +
The biggest risk is warranty disputes. If something goes wrong, the garage may blame the engine supplier, and the supplier may blame the garage. This leaves you in the middle. Specialists offer single-point liability, meaning you have one point of contact for any issues.
How do I find a reputable engine specialist- +
Look for specialists with brand-specific certifications, good reviews on Google and Trustpilot, and membership in trade bodies. Ask about their warranty handling and whether they offer fixed-fee pricing. Engines Market vets suppliers and can connect you with reputable specialists.
Is a specialist always more expensive than a local garage- +
Not always. For complex work, specialists often cost the same or less overall due to faster diagnosis and fewer comebacks. For simple repairs, a local garage is cheaper. The rule is: simple work = local garage, complex work = specialist.
What should I ask a specialist before hiring them- +
Ask about their experience with your specific engine, whether they use brand-specific diagnostic equipment, what warranty they offer (parts and labour), whether they have a fixed-fee price, and if they handle warranty claims directly. Also, check reviews and ask for examples of similar work.
