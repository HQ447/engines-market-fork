What people think vs reality

The sunk cost fallacy - why owners over-invest

❌ What many owners think

"I've had this car for years - it's worth saving no matter the cost."

✅ Reality

Every car has a financial point of no return. When repair costs approach or exceed vehicle value, you're not repairing - you're buying the car again.

The sunk cost fallacy:
• You've spent £2,000 on repairs last year
• You've owned the car for 8 years
• You're emotionally attached

None of this changes the financial reality: spending £3,500 on a £4,000 car is poor economics. [EM-OBSERVED]

"In the UK engine industry, the 50-70% rule is the dominant decision logic: if repair or replacement cost exceeds 50-70% of vehicle value, scrapping is often the better financial decision. The emotional attachment to a vehicle is the biggest obstacle to making a rational choice." [EM-OBSERVED]

Decision framework - rules-based logic

Choose with confidence

Use the frameworks below to make the right choice for your vehicle.

Choose REPAIR if:

  • ✅ Failure is minor and isolated
  • ✅ Repair cost is under 50% of vehicle value
  • ✅ Vehicle is otherwise sound
  • ✅ You plan to keep the car 2+ years
  • ✅ Vehicle has sentimental value (but only if repair <50-70% of value)
Default: Repair if cost <50% of vehicle value and vehicle otherwise sound.

Choose REPLACEMENT if:

  • ✅ Major engine failure (seized, snapped chain with head damage)
  • ✅ Vehicle worth £8,000+
  • ✅ Vehicle otherwise sound
  • ✅ Keeping car 2+ years
Default: Replacement if vehicle worth £8,000+ and otherwise sound.

Choose SCRAP if:

  • ✅ Repair/replacement >60-70% of vehicle value
  • ✅ Structural corrosion (MOT failure)
  • ✅ Multiple other advisories
  • ✅ Vehicle worth under £2,000
  • ✅ Vehicle is a write-off
  • ✅ You are ready to move on
Default: Scrap if repair/replacement >60-70% of value or structural corrosion.
