EEAT anchor - myth vs reality

What people think vs what really happens

❌ What many owners think:

"Repair is always cheaper than replacement."

✅ Reality:

Repair addresses only the visible failure. It does not address hidden wear on other components.

  • Timing chain replacement - £650-£1,800 fixes the chain. But the turbo, injectors, and oil pump remain at 100k+ miles, ready to fail.
  • Head gasket repair - £1,000-£2,500 fixes the gasket. But the bottom end has unknown bearing wear.
  • Turbo replacement - £800-£2,500 fixes the turbo. But the engine may have suffered oil contamination during the failure.

"In real UK workshop practice, repair addresses only the visible failure. Replacement eliminates all variables. The 50% rule is the dominant decision logic in the UK engine industry: if repair exceeds 50% of replacement cost, replacement is better value - particularly for vehicles worth £4,000-£15,000 and kept 2+ years." Decision framework

The 4-step decision rule

A rules-based logic to help you decide between repair and replacement. [EM-OBSERVED]

Step 1: Repair cost vs replacement cost

If repair exceeds 50% of replacement cost, replacement is often better value. [EM-OBSERVED]

Step 2: Vehicle value rule

If vehicle worth under £5,000, any repair over £800-£1,500 → consider used or scrap.

Step 3: The £4,000-£8,000 rule

If vehicle worth £4,000-£8,000 and keeping 2+ years → replacement for peace of mind.

Step 4: Ownership horizon

Keeping 2+ years → replacement eliminates future failure risk.

✅ Choose REPAIR if:

Minor and isolated failure

Sensor failure, alternator, starter, minor gasket leak

Vehicle value under £3,000

Replacement cost exceeds vehicle value

Repair cost under 50% of replacement

The 50% rule says repair is better value

Selling within 12 months

No need to invest in long-term reliability

Default recommendation: Repair (provided the failure is minor and the repair cost is under 50% of replacement cost).

⚠️ Choose REPLACEMENT (Reconditioned) if:

Major failure

Timing chain failure with head damage, seized engine, cracked block

Vehicle value £4,000-£15,000

Replacement cost is justified by vehicle value

Repair cost exceeds 50% of replacement

The 50% rule says replacement is better value

Keeping the car 2+ years

Replacement eliminates future failure risk

Multiple failures present

Chain + turbo + injectors - repair each individually adds up

High mileage (100k+)

Other components are near end-of-life

Default recommendation: Replacement (if vehicle value justifies it and you plan to keep the car 2+ years).
