Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about new vs reconditioned

Is a reconditioned engine as good as a new one- +
A reconditioned engine delivers up to 94% of the performance of a brand-new unit at 40-60% less cost. For 5-15 year old vehicles, the difference in real-world driving is often imperceptible. Quality depends on the supplier's standards.
How much does a new OEM engine cost fitted- +
A new OEM engine fitted typically costs £6,000-£24,000+ depending on engine type. A 4-cylinder family car is £3,000-£6,000+, while a V6 premium SUV can be £14,000-£24,000. Fitting adds £800-£2,200 depending on labour rates.
How much does a reconditioned engine cost fitted- +
A reconditioned engine fitted typically costs £2,000-£6,000. A 4-cylinder family car is £1,800-£3,500, while a V6 premium SUV is £5,000-£10,500. This is 40-60% less than a new OEM engine.
Which has better warranty: new or reconditioned- +
New OEM engines come with a 12-36 month manufacturer warranty. Reconditioned engines typically offer 12-24 months from the supplier. Both are substantial, but OEM warranty is often more comprehensive.
How long does it take to get a new engine- +
New OEM engines typically take 2-12 weeks to arrive, with older models often taking longer as production is phased out. Reconditioned engines are usually available in 1-4 weeks.
Is a new engine worth the extra cost- +
It depends. If your car is under warranty or an insurance job, yes. If your vehicle is 5-15 years old and out of warranty, the extra cost often exceeds the vehicle's value, and reconditioned offers better value.
What is the difference between reconditioned and remanufactured- +
Remanufactured engines are rebuilt to strict OEM standards, often including new liners, pistons, and full machining, and may be certified (e.g., BS AU 257). Reconditioned engines are also rebuilt but may use some serviceable parts. Remanufactured is typically more expensive and more thoroughly documented.
Do I need to tell my insurer about an engine change- +
Yes. Any major mechanical change, including an engine replacement, must be declared to your insurer. Failure to do so could invalidate your insurance. The new engine code and number should be recorded on your V5C and insurance policy.
Will a reconditioned engine last as long as a new one- +
With proper maintenance, a reconditioned engine can last as long as a new one. The quality of the rebuild and the care you give it are more important than whether the block is new or original.
Which option is best for a classic car- +
For a classic car, new OEM may be unavailable. Reconditioned can be a good option, but remanufactured with BS AU 257 certification is often recommended to preserve authenticity and value with documented quality.
