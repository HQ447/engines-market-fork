What this usually means in UK cars

Overheating is the engine's heart attack

Engine overheating is the single most destructive thing that can happen to your engine. When your engine overheats, metal components expand beyond their design limits. Cylinder heads warp. Head gaskets fail. Pistons seize in cylinders. Blocks crack.

In our 24,650 quote dataset, we identified five primary causes of overheating:

Coolant loss - 40% of cases

External leak or head gasket. Repair: £150-£2,000+.

Thermostat failure - 25% of cases

Stuck closed, no coolant circulation. Repair: £80-£300.

Water pump failure - 20% of cases

Pump impeller broken or leaking. Repair: £150-£700.

Radiator blockage - 10% of cases

Blocked or damaged radiator. Repair: £300-£800.

Air in system - 5% of cases

Low coolant or bleeding issue. Repair: £50-£150.

The critical rule: If your temperature gauge enters the red zone, you have seconds to minutes to stop the engine. Every second of overheating causes more damage.

What people think vs reality

The expensive mistake is thinking you can make it home

“The gauge is in the red, but I'm nearly there - I'll keep going.”
The 30 seconds you save could cost you £10,000. Stop immediately.
“I'll open the bonnet and add coolant while it's hot.”
Never open the coolant reservoir when hot. Pressurised coolant will spray out and cause severe burns.
“The fan will save it - I'll just rev it.”
Revving a hot engine increases temperature. Stop the engine immediately.
“Overheating is normal on a hot day.”
No. Your car's cooling system is designed for any UK weather. Overheating means something is broken.
“I'll just use water - it's fine.”
Water isn't a substitute for coolant. It boils at a lower temperature and lacks corrosion inhibitors.
“The engine will be fine after it cools down.”
The damage is already done. Overheating causes warping and gasket failure even if it seems fine afterwards.

⚠️ Stop driving immediately - emergency procedure

If your temperature gauge enters the red zone or you see steam:

  • Pull over safely - Find a safe location immediately
  • Turn off engine - Do not wait. Turn off the key immediately
  • Wait 30+ minutes - Do not open the bonnet. Coolant is pressurised and can cause severe burns (steam can reach 100°C+)
  • Do not add coolant - Wait for the engine to cool completely
  • Call for recovery - Do not drive. Arrange recovery to a garage

Why this is critical:

  • Opening the coolant reservoir while hot causes pressurised coolant to explode outwards
  • Coolant burns are extremely serious - the fluid is sticky and continues to burn skin
  • Steam can cause severe facial and hand burns

If you can't pull over safely:

  • Put the car in neutral
  • Turn on the heater (full heat) to draw heat from the engine
  • This gives you a few extra seconds to find a safe place to stop

Never:

  • Open the coolant reservoir when hot
  • Add cold water to a hot engine (thermal shock)
  • Keep driving "just to get home"
  • Rev the engine to "cool it down"

What to do next

Stop, cool, inspect, diagnose

  • Step 1: Stop driving (immediately)
    Pull over safely · Turn off engine · Wait 30+ minutes
  • Step 2: Check coolant level (when cold)
    Wait at least 30 minutes · Open the coolant reservoir slowly (cover with a cloth) · Check level - is it below minimum-
  • Step 3: Check for visible leaks
    Look for puddles under the car · Check hoses and radiator for damage · Look for dried coolant (white residue)
  • Step 4: Check oil (dipstick/oil filler cap)
    Look for creamy/milky substance (mayonnaise) - this confirms coolant in oil = head gasket failure
  • Step 5: Professional diagnostic (£50-£150)
    A technician will perform a cooling system pressure test, check for exhaust gases in coolant, inspect thermostat and water pump, and check radiator for blockage.

Frequently asked questions

Engine overheating FAQs

How much does engine overheating repair cost in the UK-

£80-£10,000+ depending on the cause and damage. Thermostat: £80-£300. Head gasket: £550-£2,000+. Engine replacement: £2,500-£10,000+.

Can an engine survive overheating-

Yes - if you stop immediately. If you continue driving, the engine will be destroyed.

Why is overheating so damaging-

Metal expands when hot. Cylinder heads warp, pistons seize, gaskets fail. Once the engine overheats, the damage is permanent.

What should I do if my car overheats on the motorway-

Pull over safely onto the hard shoulder. Turn off the engine immediately. Call for recovery. Do not attempt to drive to the next exit.
