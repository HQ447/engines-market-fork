The uncertainty play

A failed SDV6 crankshaft can send debris into both turbos.

The SDV6 has two turbochargers, both fed by the same oil system that carried the failed bearing and crankshaft debris. If those particles score the turbo bearings, either turbo can fail weeks or months after the engine replacement.

Path A

Fit only the reconditioned engine and keep both existing turbos.

£8,500

If one turbo fails later: add £1,500-£2,500 plus labour. If both fail, the risk doubles.

Path B

Replace both turbos preventatively at the same time as the engine.

£10,750

£8,500 engine + £2,250 for both turbos. Higher upfront cost, lower post-repair risk.

Lessons learned

For SDV6 owners, the maths is painful - but clear.

If your SDV6 is over 60,000 miles, oil analysis may detect bearing wear before failure, but this engine is also known for sudden failures. Compare independent reconditioned-engine quotes before considering a main-dealer replacement.

Preventative oil analysis£80-£120

May detect bearing wear before catastrophic failure, although SDV6 failures can happen without warning.

Reconditioned engine£7.5k-£11k

Vehicle back on the road with a usable warranty and lower cost than dealer replacement.

Main dealer new engine£20k-£30k+

Often uneconomical compared with independent specialist repair routes.

A £10,750 replacement on a £30,000 vehicle is around 36% of value - expensive but viable. A £25,000 main-dealer replacement would be around 83% and usually uneconomical.
