Case summary

A 2014 Audi A6 C7 with full independent history and an engine that drank more oil than fuel.

At 72,000 miles the owner noticed the oil warning light every 600 miles. Adding a litre became routine. Blue smoke puffed from the exhaust on cold starts. The MOT tester flagged excessive oil consumption and emissions failure. Within 6,000 miles the car was undriveable without a boot full of oil.

Early warning

The whole repair started with a litre of oil every 600 miles.

The owner dismissed it as normal — until blue smoke appeared on cold starts and the MOT tester failed the emissions. The EA888’s piston rings were allowing oil to bypass into the combustion chamber.

What owner thought“Maybe it’s just a sensor”

The car still drove normally, so the oil light didn’t feel urgent.

What it actually wasWorn piston rings

The EA888 ring design allowed excessive oil to bypass into the combustion chamber.

Why it mattersCarbon buildup & emissions

Oil burning fouled the plugs, clogged the cat, and caused MOT failure.

If your EA888 is over 70,000 miles and uses more than 1 litre of oil every 1,000 miles, book a compression test and borescope inspection. Do not wait for blue smoke or MOT failure.

Lessons learned

If your EA888 uses more than 1 litre of oil every 1,000 miles, don’t waste money on piston soaks.

The commercial lesson is simple: piston-ring symptoms are cheaper when caught early. Waiting for blue smoke or MOT failure can push the car toward dealer-level crate-engine costs.
