The uncertainty play

The engine was not the only question. Metal fragments could have damaged the turbo and other injectors.

With metal fragments throughout cylinder three, the specialist could not know whether the turbo had been damaged or the remaining injectors were at risk without further inspection.

Path A

Replace only the engine — take risk on existing turbo and remaining injectors.

£5,000

If turbo fails later: add £800–£1,500 + labour. If another injector fails: add £500–£800 per injector + potential engine damage.

Path B

Replace turbo preventatively and fit a full set of new injectors.

£6,800

Eliminates both risks. The upfront cost is higher, but downtime for future failures would cost thousands in lost revenue.

The fleet owner chose Path B. The upfront cost was higher, but downtime for a future turbo or injector failure would cost thousands in lost revenue. Key takeaway: when an injector fails catastrophically, replace the full set.
