Early warning

The clue was already in the oil.

A deep rod-knock sound is not the same as normal diesel clatter. In this case, the owner also noticed metallic glitter in the oil � a serious warning sign for bearing-shell wear.

Stage 1Cold-start knock

Deep rhythmic noise for around 10 seconds, then quieter once warm.

Stage 2Knock under load

The sound became louder and more consistent during acceleration.

Stage 3Metal in oil

Copper and silver glitter suggested bearing-shell material circulating through the oil.

If an N57 develops a deep bottom-end knock, do not keep driving it. Every journey can increase crankshaft journal scoring and reduce the chance of a repairable rebuild.

Lessons learned

The cheapest diagnostic could have changed the whole outcome.

If your N57 has over 80,000 miles, an oil analysis at the next service can flag elevated copper or lead before the crankshaft becomes unsaveable. If you already have a knock, go straight to a specialist and do not drive it.
