The first clue

A knocking noise that started faint and became terminal in two weeks.

The knock was most noticeable at idle and would quieten slightly as the engine warmed up, but never disappeared completely. The owner had been using the Amarok for regular towing - a 1,500kg plant trailer several times a week.

What they thought"Maybe a timing issue."

Hopefully not terminal. Hoping for a simple fix.

The uncertainty play

Debris contamination created risk beyond the visible failure.

When the specialist removed the oil filter and cut it open, they found significant amounts of metallic debris throughout the filter media. The turbocharger's oil supply comes directly from the main oil gallery - debris could have scored the turbo bearings.

Path A

Fit only the reconditioned engine and take the risk on existing ancillaries.

£5,500

If turbo fails later: add £1,000-£1,800 + labour. If HPFP fails later: add £800-£1,500 + labour.

Path B

Replace turbo, HPFP, and oil pump preventatively at the same time as the engine.

£7,200

£5,500 engine + £1,700 for ancillaries. Eliminates all post-repair risks.

The owner chose Path A due to budget constraints - accepting the risk of ancillary failure. The £1,700 for ancillaries was beyond their immediate budget.
Fork in the road

A reconditioned engine at £5,500 represented approximately 45-70% of the vehicle's value - expensive but viable for a well-maintained example.

Lessons learned

If your Amarok needs an engine and the dealer quotes £10,000+, get a second quote.

For a vehicle over 8 years old, a reconditioned engine with warranty is almost always better value than a new crate motor. The maths: A £12,000 dealer engine would have exceeded the vehicle's £8,000-£12,000 value. A £5,500 reconditioned engine kept the vehicle on the road economically and came with a 12-month warranty.
