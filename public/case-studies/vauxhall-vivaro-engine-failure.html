Early warning

The whole disaster started with a DPF light and a small oil patch.

The DPF light was ignored for 3 months. A small oil leak was topped up without investigation. Within weeks, the turbo seal failed and the engine seized.

What owner thought“It’ll be fine”

The van still drove, so the DPF light and oil leak did not feel urgent.

What it actually wasTurbo seal failure

The blocked DPF caused back-pressure, contributing to turbo seal failure.

Why it mattersEngine seizure

Once the turbo seal fails, oil is pumped out quickly, leading to catastrophic engine failure.

If your M9R-engined van develops a DPF warning light or an oil leak, investigate immediately. A blocked DPF can cause turbo seal failure, and a leaking turbo seal can drain the sump within weeks.

The uncertainty play

The engine was not the only question. The root cause of the turbo failure was unknown.

With the DPF blocked and the oil cooler potentially contaminated, the specialist could not know whether a new engine would suffer the same fate without addressing the root causes.

Path A

Fit reconditioned engine only. Keep existing DPF and oil cooler.

£3,500

If DPF blockage or contaminated oil cooler causes future turbo failure, additional costs will arise.

Path B

Fit reconditioned engine + new DPF + new oil cooler + new turbo.

£4,800

Eliminates all related failure points. Complete peace of mind.

The owner chose Path B — an extra £1,300 for complete peace of mind and commercial reliability. Key takeaway: when one turbo fails, the root cause may still be present. A blocked DPF or contaminated oil cooler could affect the replacement engine.
