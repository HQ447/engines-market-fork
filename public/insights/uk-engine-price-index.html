About the UK Engine Price Index 2026

This is the UK Engine Price Index 2026, a structured dataset derived from 24,650 real engine replacement quote requests submitted through EnginesMarket.co.uk between 1 January and 31 December 2025.

The index provides condition-stratified, region-normalised, and segment-banded pricing intelligence for the UK automotive aftermarket. It is designed to serve as a reference dataset for industry professionals, journalists, researchers, and vehicle owners evaluating engine replacement economics.

This index does not provide real-time quotes. For personalised pricing, use the Engines Market comparison service.

Data Classification System

Code Full Name Definition
DQD Direct Quote Dataset Raw quote requests submitted through EnginesMarket.co.uk. All quotes are customer-initiated.
SCTD Supplier Confirmed Transaction Data Quotes that progressed to confirmed supplier bookings. Cross-checked with supplier records.
PISD Post-Installation Survey Dataset Survey responses from customers 3-6 months after engine replacement. n = 100+.
EBD External Benchmark Data Third-party sources (AutoTrader, IBISWorld, industry surveys) used for contextual validation.

Data Inclusion & Exclusion Rules

✅ Included
  • Complete quote requests with valid vehicle and engine identification
  • Requests with confirmed supplier pricing
  • Requests across all UK regions
❌ Excluded
  • Duplicate submissions (same vehicle, same user, same engine, <24hrs)
  • Incomplete submissions (missing engine code, registration, or model year)
  • Dealer spam submissions
  • Requests for engines not fitting the vehicle

Price Normalisation

All prices in this index are fitted costs unless explicitly stated as supply-only. Fitted costs include:

  • Engine supply
  • Standard replacement labour (by region)
  • Consumables (fluids, filters)
  • Standard warranty coverage

Regional labour rates used for normalisation: Scotland, NI, rural: £55-£80/hr · North England, Midlands, Wales: £65-£95/hr · South England (excl. London/SE): £75-£110/hr · London & South East: £110-£160/hr

Dataset Limitations

  • Sample bias: Quote requests originate from platform users, who may not represent the broader vehicle-owning population
  • Regional weighting: London/SE is overrepresented vs. rural areas (consistent with population distribution)
  • Vehicle age skew: Older vehicles (8+ years) are overrepresented (consistent with engine failure patterns)
  • Commercial vehicles: Light commercial vehicles (Transit, Sprinter) have different economics and are not fully captured
  • Preventative replacements: A small proportion of requests (estimated <5%) are for preventative replacements

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about the UK Engine Price Index

How was the UK Engine Price Index compiled- +
The index is based on 24,650 real engine replacement quote requests submitted through EnginesMarket.co.uk between 1 January and 31 December 2025. All quotes are customer-initiated and come from real UK vehicle owners. Suppliers respond with real quoted prices. All figures are anonymised and aggregated.
What does "[H]", "[M]", and "[L]" mean- +
These are confidence scores. [H] = High confidence (n > 1,000 samples), [M] = Medium confidence (n = 200-999 samples), [L] = Low confidence (n < 200 samples). This helps users understand the statistical reliability of each data point.
Are these fitted or supply-only prices- +
All prices in this index are fitted costs unless explicitly stated as supply-only. Fitted costs include: engine supply, standard replacement labour (by region), consumables (fluids, filters), and standard warranty coverage.
How do regional labour rates affect the index- +
Geographic price variation of 20-40% exists between the North and London/SE. This is driven almost entirely by labour rate differentials, not engine supply costs. Regional labour rates used for normalisation: Scotland, NI, rural: £55-£80/hr; North England, Midlands, Wales: £65-£95/hr; South England (excl. London/SE): £75-£110/hr; London & South East: £110-£160/hr.
Why is reconditioned the most popular choice- +
Reconditioned engines account for 70.45% of all 2025 quote requests. This is due to the balance of cost and warranty. Reconditioned engines are disassembled, cleaned, and have worn parts replaced (pistons, rings, bearings, seals). They come with a 6-12 month warranty and are tested to standard.
What's the forecast for engine prices in 2026- +
The 2026 forecast shows used engines increasing 5-8% (premium used +6-8%, mainstream +5-6%), reconditioned increasing ~3%, and rebuilt increasing 2-3%. Key drivers include tighter used engine supply, continued labour inflation (3-5% annually), and strong demand as average vehicle age reaches 8.4 years.
How often is the index updated- +
Price bands are updated quarterly, while market share, trend analysis, and forecasts are updated annually. The next full review is scheduled for January 2027.
Can I use this data for research or journalism- +
Yes. The UK Engine Price Index is designed for industry professionals, journalists, researchers, and vehicle owners. For academic/journalistic citation: "UK Engine Price Index 2026, Engines Market (accessed [date]). Dataset based on 24,650 UK engine replacement quote requests, 2025." For data access inquiries, contact data@enginesmarket.co.uk.
How does this compare to other benchmarks- +
Our index aligns closely with industry benchmarks. Compared to WhoCanFixMyCar (2025), our index is ~5% lower. Compared to AutoTrader (2025), our used engine costs are ~7% lower. Labour rate benchmarks from IBISWorld (2025) show our £68.51/hr average is within the £67-£72/hr range.
What are the limitations of the dataset- +
Key limitations include: sample bias (platform users may not represent the broader population), regional weighting (London/SE overrepresented), vehicle age skew (older vehicles overrepresented), limited commercial vehicle data, and a small proportion of preventative replacements (<5%).
